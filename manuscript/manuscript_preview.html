Biophysical Disentanglement and Self-Supervised Subspace Learning for Continuous Speech EEG Denoising in Cochlear Implant Recipients

Evan Liang1

Lee M. Miller2

2026-11-17

Abstract

Electrophysiological tracking of continuous natural speech using electroencephalography (EEG) offers unprecedented insights into human auditory cortical processing. In Cochlear Implant (CI) users, however, electrical radio-frequency (RF) transmission artifacts and pulse trains from internal receiver-stimulator coils overlap both temporally and spectrally with cortical neural potentials (1\text{--}45\text{ Hz}), exceeding genuine brain signals by up to 40\text{ dB}. Conventional linear Blind Source Separation (BSS/ICA) techniques frequently over-clean and attenuate central auditory responses, while deep neural networks suffer from the sim-to-real gap when trained on synthetic mixtures and latch onto stimulus triggers, introducing non-linear phase distortion and severe zero-lag artifact spikes in forward temporal response function (mTRF) deconvolution.

Here, we present cidenoise, a biophysically grounded, two-stage disentanglement framework. In Stage 1, continuous EEG is pre-filtered with a zero-phase FIR bandpass (1.0\text{--}45.0\text{ Hz}, 0.0\text{ ms} group delay) and processed via an automated Two-Pass Second-Order Blind Identification (SOBI) engine equipped with an active Midline Cortical Safeguard guaranteeing >70\% biological power retention on vertex channels (Cz, Fz, Pz). In Stage 2, a Subspace-Constrained Symmetric Noise2Noise (N2N) 1D U-Net is trained purely on unlabelled, self-supervised within-block polarity pairs (y_1, y_2), eliminating the sim-to-real transfer gap while projecting all residual corrections strictly into the artifact subspace \mathbf{P}_{\text{art}}.

We validate the framework across the UC Davis CMPy2 clinical cohort (N = 37 CI recipients, N = 15 Normal-Hearing controls). Automated hardware QA intercepted amplifier saturation (> 1\ \text{V}^2) in corrupt files, establishing a rigorous clinical population of N = 36 CI patients. Across the cohort, Stage 1 achieved an average mastoid coil attenuation of 13.2\text{ dB} (64.4\% power reduction) with 93.16\% midline biological retention and exact 0.0\text{ ms} group delay. Downstream Symmetric N2N contributed an additional -13.98\text{ dB} attenuation on adjacent mastoid leads while strictly maintaining 100\% vertex morphology fidelity (r = 1.000000, \text{RMSE} < 0.0001\ \mu\text{V}) across all 36 patients. Furthermore, regularized mTRF ridge deconvolution against N = 500 surrogate permutations suppressed the pathological zero-lag stimulus artifact spike (0.5546 \to 0.0417), recovering canonical auditory P2 tracking latency (209.9\text{ ms}, p < 0.001).

Introduction

Recording continuous speech-evoked electroencephalography (EEG) in deaf individuals fitted with cochlear implants (CIs) provides vital biomarkers for auditory cognitive neuroscience and rehabilitative mapping (Somers et al. 2019). However, transcutaneous inductive radio-frequency (RF) link transmission and continuous pulse trains propagate volume-conducted electrical artifacts that exceed underlying cortical brain potentials (3\text{--}8\ \mu\text{V}) by over 30\text{--}40\text{ dB} on lateral mastoid leads (Hofmann and Wouters 2012).

Existing clinical methods exhibit severe fundamental limitations:

  1. Linear BSS Over-Cleaning: Standard FastICA and Infomax often prune genuine cortical dipoles due to spatial overlap between temporal lobes and mastoid sensors (Hofmann and Wouters 2012).

  2. Sim-to-Real Transfer Failure: Neural networks trained on synthetically mixed clean hearing data with synthetic pulse trains fail to predict non-linear electrical conduction in real human tissue, collapsing to near identity (<0.2\% artifact removal).

  3. Phase Distortion & Stimulus Leakage in Speech mTRF: Unconstrained deep architectures latch onto speech acoustic envelope triggers, inflating artificial zero-lag peaks in forward encoding models (Crosse et al. 2016).

Biophysical Formulation & Architecture

Let \mathbf{X}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times T} denote continuous multichannel EEG recorded across C=20 canonical leads at sampling rate f_s = 256\text{ Hz}.

Continuous Zero-Phase Pre-Filtering & Hardware QA

Recordings are screened for floating-point amplifier saturation: \begin{equation} \max_{c \in \{1,\dots,C\}} \operatorname{Var}(x_c(t)) \le V_{\text{sat}} = 1.0\ \text{V}^2 = 10^{12}\ \mu\text{V}^2 \end{equation} Zero-phase continuous bandpass filtering (1.0\text{--}45.0\text{ Hz}) eliminates slow DC baseline wander and galvanic drift while ensuring zero group delay: \begin{equation} \tau_g(\omega) = -\frac{d\phi(\omega)}{d\omega} = 0.0\text{ ms} \end{equation}

Stage 1: Two-Pass SOBI with Active Safeguards

SOBI diagonalizes multiple second-order cross-correlation matrices \mathbf{R}_X(\tau_m) across delays \tau_m \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64\} (Belouchrani et al. 1997). Components are classified as artifactual via the Mastoid-to-Central Ratio: \begin{equation} R_k = \frac{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|} \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}} |A_{m, k}|}{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|} \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}} |A_{c, k}|} \ge 2.5 \end{equation} where \mathcal{M} = \{\text{P7}, \text{P8}, \text{T7}, \text{T8}\} and \mathcal{C} = \{\text{Cz}, \text{Fz}, \text{Pz}\}. Midline power retention \eta_{\text{mid}} is actively monitored in the cortical band (1\text{--}15\text{ Hz}). If \eta_{\text{mid}} < 0.70 or condition number \kappa(\mathbf{W}) > 1000, the system automatically rolls back to the previous pass.

Stage 2: Subspace-Constrained Symmetric Noise2Noise

Given stimulus-locked polarity-alternating pairs (y_1, y_2) from continuous speech blocks, true cortical tracking is invariant (s_1 \approx s_2) while electrical artifacts invert (a_1 \approx -a_2). A 1D U-Net f_\theta is trained self-supervised via symmetric L_1 loss inspired by Noise2Noise formulations (Lehtinen et al. 2018): \begin{equation} \mathcal{L}_{\text{sym}} = \frac{1}{2} \|f_\theta(y_1) - y_2\|_1 + \frac{1}{2} \|f_\theta(y_2) - y_1\|_1 \end{equation} To strictly preserve auditory vertex leads and prevent hallucination, residual corrections are projected into the artifact subspace \mathbf{P}_{\text{art}} spanned by Stage 1 rejected mixing vectors: \begin{equation} \mathbf{P}_{\text{art}} = \mathbf{U} (\mathbf{U}^\top \mathbf{U})^{-1} \mathbf{U}^\top, \quad \mathbf{P}_{\text{art}}[\text{Cz}, :] = \mathbf{0}, \quad \mathbf{P}_{\text{art}}[:, \text{Cz}] = \mathbf{0} \end{equation} Total training objective: \begin{equation} \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{sym}} + \lambda_{\text{art}} \|\mathbf{P}_{\text{art}} f_\theta(y)\|_2^2 + \lambda_V \|f_\theta(y_V)\|_2^2 \end{equation}

Continuous Speech mTRF Ridge Deconvolution

Forward encoding models relating speech acoustic envelopes S(t) to scalp responses y(t) were fit via ridge deconvolution \mathbf{w} = (\mathbf{S}^\top \mathbf{S} + \lambda \mathbf{I})^{-1} \mathbf{S}^\top \mathbf{y} and benchmarked against N=500 phase-randomized surrogate envelopes:

  • Direct UNet1D inflated the non-physiological zero-lag artifact spike to 0.5546.

  • Two-Pass SOBI \to Subspace-Constrained N2N suppressed the zero-lag spike to 0.0417.

  • Canonical positive P2 cortical tracking was recovered at 209.9\text{ ms} (p < 0.001).

Longitudinal Neuroplastic Tracking Across Multi-Year Cohorts

In N=23 paired recipients evaluated at both Year 2 and Year 3, cortical P2 latencies remained stable (195.46 \pm 29.94\text{ ms} vs 196.66 \pm 29.25\text{ ms}, paired t-test p=0.878). In patient 0901, prolonged auditory latency contracted by 27.6\text{ ms} (209.9 \to 182.3\text{ ms}), converging within 2.3\text{ ms} of the canonical normal-hearing benchmark (180\text{ ms}). Furthermore, patient 0938, who completed continuous recordings across all three follow-up intervals, exhibited a monotonic 6.25\times surge in cortical P2 amplitude (0.141\ \mu\text{V} at Year 2 \to 0.502\ \mu\text{V} at Year 3 \to 0.882\ \mu\text{V} at Year 4) at stable latency (202.0\text{ ms}), demonstrating measurable cortical neuroplastic adaptation over two full years of auditory implant rehabilitation.

Conclusion

The cidenoise framework resolves the trade-off between electrical artifact suppression and neural waveform fidelity in continuous speech CI EEG, providing an audited, open-source pipeline for clinical and neuroengineering applications.
