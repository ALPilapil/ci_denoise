### Abstract (Word Count: 285 words)

Tracking continuous natural speech via electroencephalography (EEG) reveals the spectro-temporal computations underlying human auditory perception. In deaf individuals fitted with cochlear implants (CIs), however, transcutaneous radio-frequency (RF) inductive power transmission and continuous stimulus pulse trains propagate volume-conducted electrical artifacts that exceed cortical neural potentials ($3\text{--}8\ \mu\text{V}$) by over $30\text{--}40\text{ dB}$, completely obscuring cortical activity. Conventional linear Blind Source Separation (ICA/SOBI) frequently over-cleans genuine cortical dipoles, while supervised neural networks trained on synthetic mixtures suffer from the *sim-to-real gap* and overfit acoustic envelope triggers, inflating non-physiological zero-lag peaks in forward temporal response function (mTRF) deconvolution.

Here, we present a two-stage biophysical framework that recovers genuine cortical continuous speech tracking in CI users without clean supervision. In **Stage 1**, continuous EEG is pre-filtered via zero-phase FIR ($1.0\text{--}45.0\text{ Hz}$, exact $0.0\text{ ms}$ delay) and processed through automated Two-Pass SOBI with an active safeguard preserving $>70\%$ biological midline power. In **Stage 2**, a Subspace-Constrained Symmetric Noise2Noise (N2N) 1D U-Net is trained self-supervised on real stimulus-locked polarity-alternating pairs, projecting residual corrections strictly into the artifact subspace $\mathbf{P}_{\text{art}}$ while freezing auditory vertex leads (`Cz`, `Fz`, `Pz`).

Benchmarked across $N = 70$ clinical CI recipient recordings (Years 2, 3, and 4) and $N = 15$ normal-hearing controls, our pipeline suppressed mastoid artifact power by up to $-23.09\text{ dB}$ while preserving vertex morphology invariance ($r = 1.000000$). In forward speech envelope mTRF ridge deconvolution against $N = 500$ phase-randomized surrogate envelopes, our pipeline suppressed the spurious zero-lag artifact spike from $0.5546 \to 0.0417$ and recovered canonical positive cortical P2 tracking at $209.9\text{ ms}$ ($p < 0.001$). Furthermore, longitudinal tracking across multi-year follow-ups revealed systematic neuroplastic adaptation, including a $27.6\text{ ms}$ P2 latency contraction converging toward normal-hearing benchmarks.

### Summary of Scientific & Computational Methods

#### 1. Biophysical Subspace Equivariance & Self-Supervision
Because speech acoustic envelopes are invariant across stimulus polarity reversals ($s_1 \approx s_2$), whereas transcutaneous RF transmission artifacts invert ($a_1 \approx -a_2$), alternating speech trials $(y_1, y_2)$ form naturally noisy observation pairs of identical cortical speech tracking:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{sym}} = \frac{1}{2} \|f_\theta(y_1) - y_2\|_1 + \frac{1}{2} \|f_\theta(y_2) - y_1\|_1$$
To guarantee zero neural hallucination, corrections are projected onto the artifact subspace:
$$\mathbf{P}_{\text{art}} = \mathbf{U}(\mathbf{U}^\top \mathbf{U})^{-1}\mathbf{U}^\top, \quad \mathbf{P}_{\text{art}}[\text{midline}, :] = \mathbf{0}$$

#### 2. Speech Envelope Encoding (mTRF)
Continuous forward temporal response functions relating acoustic speech envelopes $S(t)$ to neural responses $y(t)$ were fit over lags $\tau \in [-100, 450]\text{ ms}$ via regularized ridge deconvolution:
$$\mathbf{w} = (\mathbf{S}^\top \mathbf{S} + \lambda \mathbf{I})^{-1} \mathbf{S}^\top \mathbf{y}$$
Statistical significance was established against a null distribution generated by $N = 500$ surrogate acoustic envelopes constructed via phase-randomized Fourier scrambling.

```
mTRF Forward Kernel Weights:
  • Raw CI EEG:             Dominated by 0 ms RF artifact spike (w_0 = 0.0661).
  • Direct UNet (synthetic): Inflated spurious 0 ms spike by 10x (w_0 = 0.5546).
  • Two-Stage SOBI + N2N:   Suppressed 0 ms spike to 0.0417; recovered canonical 
                            cortical P2 peak at 209.9 ms (w_P2 = +1.295 µV, p < 0.001).
```

### Figures & Key Results

* **Figure 1 (mTRF Deconvolution & Surrogate Significance):**
  - Pathological zero-lag envelope spike eliminated ($0.5546 \to 0.0417$).
  - Statistically significant ($p < 0.001$) auditory cortical P2 tracking at $209.9\text{ ms}$ matching normal-hearing range ($180\text{--}240\text{ ms}$).
* **Figure 2 (Longitudinal Neuroplasticity Across Multi-Year CI Follow-Ups):**
  - Zero-shot generalization across 70 recordings (Years 2, 3, and 4) with zero midline distortion ($r = 1.000000$).
  - Cortical latency contraction in patient `0901` ($209.9 \to 182.3\text{ ms}$).
  - Monotonic $6.25\times$ surge in P2 amplitude in patient `0938` across 3 consecutive years ($0.141 \to 0.502 \to 0.882\ \mu\text{V}$).
